# Design Spec v2 — SUQUIA Photography Portfolio

Supersedes v1. Reflects the decisions made over the mockup iterations (canvas: three approved artboards — Landing, Album carousel, Album page).

## Concept statement

**A white, quiet, editorial page where the photographs are the only color.** Serif typography in a grayscale hierarchy, generous whitespace, and motion as the personality layer: an inverted name over drifting photos, an endless calm carousel, a tube-roll scroll. Nothing competes with the images: no cropping, no symmetric grids, no cursor-following gimmicks.

## Art direction

**Color** — Background near-white `#FAFAFA`. UI text grayscale only: `#111` primary, `#666`–`#888` secondary, `#B0B0B0`–`#CCC` tertiary. No accent color — the photos are the accent.

**Typography** — Serif, in the tamakiyoshida direction: classical but softer than Times. **EB Garamond** (Google Fonts; weights 400/500/600 + italic) is the working face; fallback stack `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`. Usage rules: titles in Title Case ("Alpes", not "ALPES") at weight 500; photo titles in italic ("Ibón de Plan"); small labels uppercase with wide tracking (0.16–0.22em) at 12–13px; numbers at 14px, `#666`. Importance is expressed by size + gray value, never by weight ramping.

**Brand** — The name **SUQUIA** appears: huge on the landing (~158px, weight 500), small in headers/footer (13px, tracked uppercase). No monogram/mark. Primary nav labels: `ABOUT ME`, `ALBUMS` top-left, `ATLAS` top-right (split from the other two — a view over the whole archive, not a section); one rotated 90° edge label, `CREDITS`, on the landing's left edge (a deliberate asymmetry — see §1).

**Layout principles** — Photos always at natural aspect ratio, never cropped (the only exception: carousel slivers, which are teasers by design). Organic scatter, not grids. Every photo fits within one viewport height (~max 85vh) — never larger than the screen.

## Page structure

### 1. Landing
**A tall scrolling composition (~250–300vh), smoothed with Lenis — superseded from an earlier "single fixed screen" draft.** The page genuinely scrolls; reference: gregorcollienne.com. **SUQUIA stays fixed in the center of the viewport for the whole scroll**, as do the nav, rotated edge labels, and counter — none of them are part of the scrolling layer. The photos (9–10 of them, mostly favorites, one or two acting as album shortcuts) live in the scrolling layer and pass behind/around the fixed name as the page scrolls; spaced so 2–3 are on screen at a time, natural aspect ratios always, each ≤ 70vh. On desktop every photo keeps clear whitespace around it (min ~60px to its neighbors); at narrow/compressed widths some overlap is acceptable. Each photo and its number (`01`, `02`, …) are wrapped in the same container so they move together in sync — the number sits just outside the photo edge (below-left), never covered. **SUQUIA renders white with `mix-blend-mode: difference`** — reads near-black on the `#FAFAFA` background at rest and inverts wherever a photo passes under it. This depends on strict stacking-context hygiene: no `transform`/`opacity`/`filter`/`will-change`/`perspective` on the fixed name element's ancestors, or on the scrolling layer's own wrapper — only individual photos animate, never the name or anything between it and the page root. (This exact setup broke once already from an ancestor transform; verify on load, with no photo under it, that the name reads black before treating this as done.) Directly beneath the name, ~30px below its baseline: a fixed, centered descriptor line, `SELECTED PHOTOGRAPHS THROUGH THE YEARS`, 13px, letter-spacing 0.34em, plain `#888888` — a sibling of the `<h1>`, not text inside it, so it never inherits the blend. It never scrolls and is never covered by a photo at any scroll position (guaranteed by z-index alone: it sits in the same z-30 layer as the name, above the z-10 photo layer, regardless of horizontal position — no `LANDING_LAYOUT` exclusion needed the way the name's own blend band needs one). Chrome: `ABOUT ME` and `ALBUMS` top-left, `ATLAS` top-right (13px, tracking 0.22em, `#111111` resting — the same treatment as the other two; deliberately split from them since Atlas is a view over the whole archive, not a section like the other two), a single rotated `CREDITS` label on the *left* edge (clear of the photos, links to `/credits`), `01 / 06` counter bottom — all fixed, no scroll hint. (A matching `MENU` label was removed — it drove no overlay or state, just a label with nothing behind it. `CREDITS` alone is a deliberate asymmetry now, not a leftover; don't add a label back on the other edge to "balance" it. `PHOTOGRAPHS — {year}` was also removed from the top-right — non-interactive text sitting among interactive links invited clicks that went nowhere, and the descriptor now does that labeling job.) The ALBUMS nav link is the only way into the carousel; the landing scroll never leads there.

### 2. Album carousel (its own view — NOT reached by scrolling the landing) — BUILT, `src/motion/AlbumsCarousel.tsx`
Opened only via the `ALBUMS` nav link. **Infinite and circular** — the strip loops; there is no first or last position (indices are `mod`'d into the album list, not clamped). Anatomy as actually implemented:

- **Slides**: constant height (500px) always, for every slide, at every moment — nothing ever scales vertically. A folded slide is a fixed, equal-width sliver (110px) regardless of its album's aspect ratio; the open (active) slide's width is its cover's own natural width at that height (`height × aspect ratio`) — landscape covers open wide, vertical covers open as a tall panel, and the width animates between those two states, never the height. Covers are never cropped.
- **Commit is position-triggered, not velocity-triggered**: scroll/drag feed a velocity that integrates into an offset; crossing one slot's pitch (sliver width + gap) advances the active index by one and the offset carries the remainder — no minimum speed, no separate "flick" threshold. Below that distance the strip simply follows the input 1:1 and holds wherever it's left, with **no snap-back or rubber-banding** — recentring happens only as a side effect of an actual commit, never while idle.
- **Direction-based edge anchoring**: advancing forward pins the open slide's RIGHT edge in place and reveals/grows it toward the left (the image is pinned `right:0`, cropped/revealed from its left side); advancing backward mirrors this exactly (LEFT edge pinned, grows right, image pinned `left:0`). Both the outgoing and incoming slide in a given step always share the same anchor side, so a commit reads as one continuous motion rather than two independent folds.
- **Rest position is two fixed lines, not the viewport centre**: `X_RIGHT`/`X_LEFT` are computed once from the *average* natural cover width across all albums and never move. A forward commit settles with the open slide's right edge exactly on `X_RIGHT`; a backward commit settles with its left edge exactly on `X_LEFT`. The open photo's own centre therefore varies a little album to album (narrower covers rest right-of-centre, wider ones left-of-centre) — accepted as the natural consequence of a genuinely static anchor edge, not corrected for.
- **Frozen image anchoring**: which edge a slide's `<img>` is pinned to is decided once — when that slide starts folding or unfolding, or when it first mounts if it's never been opened — and held fixed for as long as it stays folded. A settled sliver's crop never changes again on its own; only the slide actually transitioning right now may pick a new anchor.
- **Depth cues**: blur and a dark veil scale by distance from the active index (a few fixed steps, not a continuous gradient) — the active slide has neither.
- **No native scroll on either axis** — the page and the strip are both `overflow: hidden`; all motion is transform-driven, never real scroll position.
- **Navigation**: only the open (active) slide is a link, to `/album/[slug]` — folded slivers do nothing on click, only step the carousel via the normal drag/scroll physics. Hovering the open slide nudges the info-block title ~4px right and grows a thin rule under it (300ms, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`); nothing on the photo itself ever moves or transforms. This is a plain client-side route change today — the shared-element "jump into" expansion (Motion inventory #4) is not built yet.

Info bottom-left (album title serif ~30px + gray meta line), pagination bottom-right (`01 / 04`).

### 3. Album page
**No hero.** The carousel's jump-into transition (center slide expands, GSAP Flip) IS the entrance; the page opens on a typographic title block: small `ALBUM 01 — 12 PHOTOS`, title serif ~110px, location/year line. Then a vertical top-down flow: photos at natural ratios, each ≤ one viewport height, alternating placement (centered / offset-left / offset-right), each with info alongside. **Caption hierarchy — place outranks technical data:**
```
01   San Miguel Street   ·   San Miguel de Allende, México
                              43MM — F/5.6 — ISO 100
```
Number (`#BBBBBB`), italic serif title (`#111111`), and place (`#666666`, "City, Country" — never coordinates, at any precision; that's an atlas-hover thing, not a caption thing) share one baseline row; capture data (focal length — aperture — ISO) drops to its own line below at 12px `#A8A8A8`, the footnote. Wraps gracefully at narrow widths. Right-edge fixed control: SVG chevron up / `01 / 12` counter / chevron down for prev/next photo navigation. Ends with a NEXT ALBUM block (small cover + title) and a minimal footer (SUQUIA — INSTAGRAM — EMAIL — year).

### 4. About and Credits — two separate pages, not one
Split after the original single-page plan proved too cramped for both a personal bio and a colophon; each now has its own route.

**4a. `/about`** — One column, generous whitespace, same typographic system, no images competing (an optional portrait slot exists but stays commented out until a real photo is supplied). Contents: a short bio in EB Garamond; contact structured as its own labelled block (email as plain selectable text with a `mailto:` link — no contact form, no backend, no third-party mail service; the block is scoped so a form could replace the email later without touching anything else on the page) and a separate Instagram block in the same label+value pattern. Same "← Suquia" back-link chrome as the album page. Reachable from the header nav (`ABOUT ME`).

**4b. `/credits`** — A colophon, not a page about the photographer. Small quiet type, one column, plenty of air, no heading shouting "Credits" in large type — the page should read as a footnote. Exactly five lines: the photography/design/development credit, the typeface, the "built with" stack, a GitHub repository link, and a rights line ("All photographs © Javier Suquia, {year}. Not to be reproduced without permission.", year from one computed constant). Not in the header nav — reached via the landing's rotated `CREDITS` edge label (see §1's chrome note: `MENU` was removed as a dead label, so `CREDITS` is now the site's only rotated edge label, a deliberate asymmetry).

### 5. Atlas (`/atlas`) — a map over the whole archive, reached from the header's `ATLAS` link

**Not the tube-roll site, not a gallery.** A schematic world map, hand-rolled with no map library and no GL — the header (`Atlas` serif title + a meta line, counts computed from `photos.ts` at render time, never hardcoded: `{N} Photographs — {N} Cities — {N} Countries`) sits above a full-bleed map view (`src/motion/Atlas.tsx`).

- **Projection** — equirectangular, `x = (lng+180)/360 · mapWidth`, `y = (90-lat)/180 · mapHeight`. Preserves aspect ratio, so degree-spacing reads as uniform screen-spacing at any zoom.
- **Landmass** — a static, pre-computed dot grid (`src/data/atlas-land-dots.json`, generated by `scripts/generate-atlas-dots.mjs` from Natural Earth 110m land data, Public Domain — see `docs/atlas-data-sources.md`), `#E2E2E2`, ~2.2px, counter-scaled to stay a constant screen size at every zoom. No runtime geometry processing.
- **Marks** — one per photo that has coordinates; photos without are simply omitted, no placeholder. `precision: 'exact'` → solid filled dot, `#111111`, r≈5px. `precision: 'city'` → unfilled ring, 1px `#111111` stroke, r≈7px (rendered with `fill="transparent"`, not `"none"`, so the whole disc — not just the 1px stroke line — is hoverable/clickable). A centroid is never drawn as a precise point at a zoom where the difference would read as one. One-line legend, caption grey, bottom-left.
- **Initial framing** — the bounding box of photos that actually have coordinates, plus a generous margin, computed from the data so it reframes itself as the archive grows. Never opens on the whole globe; "World View" (bottom-right) resets to it on demand.
- **Pan/zoom** — no library: one `{scale, offsetX, offsetY}` transform on the map's `<g>`, driven by wheel (zooms toward the cursor, not the center), drag, and two-finger pinch. Panning is clamped to the map's bounds. Zoom range runs from "world fits" to roughly "a city fills the view." Marks within ~14 screen px of each other cluster into one numbered mark at low zoom, separating as the view zooms in; clicking a cluster zooms toward it.
- **Hover** — shows the photo small beside its mark, with `CITY · COUNTRY`, and for `'exact'` marks only, DMS coordinates. Clicking a single mark navigates to that photo inside its album (`/album/[slug]#photo-N`).
- **Motion** — no tube/roll GL here (this is a map, not the gallery); `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the hover-preview's fade transition but keeps the reveal itself, and never moves the map on its own.

## Motion inventory (7 effects — #1-6 frozen from the original approved set; #7, the nav-label scramble, added later as a small hover-only polish pass, not a departure from "frozen")

1. **Tube/roll scroll** (Lenis + WebGL2 via `ogl`, not GSAP ScrollTrigger — superseded after prototyping in `/lab/tube`) — every photo on a page is painted onto one shared canvas (`src/motion/GLSurface.tsx`, which mirrors the page's normal `<img>` elements rather than replacing them) and bends around a cylinder belonging to the *viewport*, not any individual photo: zero displacement at the screen's vertical center, maximum at the top/bottom edges, driven by scroll velocity and direction-independent (scrolling up and down bend the same way). Site-wide signature, but with per-instance intensity — spectacle-level on the landing, barely perceptible on album pages (the work should be looked at there, not performed at) — via the same component's `bendDepth`/`bendRadiusMultiplier` props. Falls back to the plain DOM/CSS photos if a WebGL context can't be created; `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps GL active but pins the bend flat. The carousel does not use this — its motion model is independent (see §2). On the landing, Lenis smooths the real page scroll and each photo also gets a modest depth-based parallax on top of it (unchanged, still CSS transform, independent of the GL layer); the fixed name is never part of that transformed layer.
2. **Hover on images** — inner zoom/shift within a fixed frame; boundaries never move.
3. **Infinite carousel** — circular drag/scroll strip, slivers + open center.
4. **Jump-into transition** — carousel center expands into the album page (GSAP Flip / View Transitions).
5. **Rolling digits** — pagination and photo counters animate per-digit.
6. **Entrance reveals** — on the landing only, on first load and every soft-navigation back to `/`: every photo starts stacked at the viewport centre, hidden, and eases out (750ms, ease-out cubic, staggered ~10ms apart from the centre of the composition outward — total span ~900ms) to its layout position. The starting state — `opacity: 0` plus the centre-ward offset — is declared directly in the photo's JSX (computed analytically from its own layout data, in vw/vh units, not measured) so it's present in the very first rendered HTML, server markup included; no client effect has to run first for it to be true, so no frame can ever show a photo already at its final position before the entrance starts. The parallax offset and the entrance offset live on two separate nested wrapper elements (outer = parallax, always live from frame one; inner = entrance, animates to exactly `translate(0,0)` and is then left alone) so the two transforms compose automatically instead of one having to hand off to the other. Order is name first, then photos: the entrance is gated on the wordmark's own arrival lock-in landing (see below), not on texture-readiness directly — though that lock-in itself never resolves before every above-the-fold photo's texture has actually been uploaded (`readyNodeCount` on `GLSurface`), so photos still never reveal before their textures exist; they simply wait one extra beat for the name to settle first. The GLSurface tube/roll renderer reads each mirrored node's *parent's* `opacity` as a per-plane alpha multiplier each frame (`uOpacity`, fragment-shader-side) — a DOM `opacity: 0` alone hides only the `<img>`, not what the canvas paints from its live rect, so this is required for the hidden state to actually be invisible on screen, not just in the DOM. `prefers-reduced-motion`: no travel, photos simply fade in (opacity only) at their final position, gated on texture-readiness directly — there's no wordmark animation to sequence against under reduced motion, so this path stays independent of it.
   The wordmark plays its own arrival BEFORE the photos (`src/motion/DecipherWordmark.tsx`) — its scramble IS the page's own loading indicator, no spinner needed. Built on one primitive, a DECIPHER: a six-letter word resolving out of noise into a target word, left to right, one position at a time — a signal locking in, not a mechanical drum (no rotation, no clipping window, no crossfade layers).
   **The primitive**: each position cycles through random letters — drawn ONLY from the letters of Javi's own name (J A V I E R S U Z Q; no other letter, symbol, or digit ever appears — even the noise is his name) — then locks on its target letter; positions lock left to right, ~50ms apart. A position whose letter is IDENTICAL between the source and target word never scrambles at all — it's already "locked", which is how "SU" visibly stays put while only "QUIA" turns on SUAREZ->SUQUIA, and how the "E" JAVIER and SUAREZ share stays put on that turn (do not animate a letter into an identical copy of itself). One full transition — noise to every position locked — takes ~400ms: ~18 glyph changes per second per position while actively cycling (a decode readout, not a flicker), cascaded so the LAST position's own cycle-then-lock window still finishes within that same ~400ms total (the same "a position's own cascade slot plus its own duration equals the transition's total" arithmetic used throughout this codebase's timed sequences).
   **Widths — geometric, not just timing** (`src/motion/DecipherWordmark.tsx`): EB Garamond's capitals are proportional, not monospaced — at the wordmark's real size the widest letter (Q, ~120px) is ~2.2x the narrowest (I, ~55px), so a slot sized for one glyph can badly overflow into its neighbours when a random wide letter lands in it (measured worst case before this was addressed: ~33px overflow on each side). Two things fix this together, neither alone: (1) each position's own box width — not just its glyph — tweens smoothly from its source letter's width to its target's, on its own per-position clock (ending exactly at that position's own lock moment, same cascade as the glyph), never snapping to match whatever random glyph is currently showing (that would jitter the word at the scramble rate instead of it breathing once per transition); (2) the random pool a position draws from every tick is filtered to letters whose own measured width is `>= 0.6x` and `<= 1.0x` the slot's width AT THAT INSTANT — narrower letters are fine (never overflow), but nothing wider than the slot itself is ever drawn, which is what actually caps overlap between adjacent glyphs at a sub-pixel rounding fraction rather than the ~10-12% a looser upper ratio would still permit (confirmed by a per-frame verification: a `Range` around each rendered character's text node, measured via `getBoundingClientRect()` for its true ink extent, checked that consecutive glyphs' boxes never overlap by more than ~0.7px across full arrival and hover runs). Width and glyph-pick both read from ONE analytic function of elapsed time (not a CSS transition sampled by a separate clock) and are driven by the SAME requestAnimationFrame loop — an earlier version used CSS transitions for width and a `setInterval` for glyph cycling; under real load `setInterval` can fire meaningfully later than its nominal delay, letting a letter chosen as valid go stale before its next scheduled redraw, which is what caused the worst overlaps during testing. Letter widths (both the three target words' own, and all ten alphabet letters' own) are measured at runtime via `offsetWidth` from hidden reference spans — never hardcoded — re-measured on mount, on window resize (the wordmark's `clamp()`-based font-size means real pixel widths shift with viewport width), and once `document.fonts.ready` resolves (a measurement taken against a fallback face before EB Garamond has actually loaded would be wrong). At rest, each position's width is exactly its letter's own natural width, so the resting word is pixel-identical to the plain static text — `font-kerning: none` is set on the wordmark in every state, static text included, specifically because without it the plain word's own kerned width would be very slightly narrower than the sum of its individually-measured, kerning-off letters, breaking that pixel-identity. The six position elements are freshly mounted at the START of every run (a moment before, this was plain static text) — a smooth tween needs a genuine starting value to animate from, which a brand-new element doesn't have, so a run's very first transition seeds each position at the SOURCE word's own width (zero visible shift at the mount itself) before the rAF loop takes over animating toward the real target; every subsequent transition within the same run needs no such seeding, since its position elements already exist by then. Once every position has locked for the final time, the whole per-position structure swaps for the plain static "SUQUIA" text node — same DOM as the non-animated wordmark, selectable, identical for layout. Positions are `aria-hidden`; the wordmark's accessible name comes from `aria-label="Suquia"` on the `<h1>` itself, stable regardless of which DOM is currently inside it.
   **Arrival — the decipher as the loading state**: the wordmark starts scrambling (name-alphabet noise, no defined target yet) the instant it's triggered, before anything about the photos is known — that, plus the still-piled photos, IS the loading state. It keeps scrambling indefinitely while textures are loading; the moment the scene is ready, it plays exactly one transition, resolving to SUQUIA (the ~400ms lock-in, left to right). Only once that lock-in finishes do the photos leave the pile — the wordmark's own `onArrived` callback is what the photo-entrance effect actually waits on, not texture-readiness directly. If everything is ready near-instantly, the arrival still plays a short decipher rather than jump-cutting to static text: a minimum 300ms of scramble always precedes the lock-in, ~700ms total — a deliberate reveal, not an artificial wait.
   **Sequencing, inverted from before**: photos wait on the wordmark now, not the other way around. `arriveKey` (bumped once per arrival — a sentinel-0-until-triggered counter, same pattern used everywhere in this codebase for a replayable child) tells the wordmark to start scrambling; its `onArrived(arriveKey)` callback reports back which arrival's lock-in just finished. The photo-entrance effect compares that against its own current `arriveKey` (`nameLockedKey === arriveKey`) rather than tracking a separately-reset boolean — a freshly bumped `arriveKey` alone already makes that comparison false the instant a new arrival starts, with nothing to explicitly reset, sidestepping an ordering hazard a reset-based boolean would otherwise have on a return visit (a layout effect resetting a "locked" flag and a sibling effect reading it in the same commit can observe stale state depending on declaration order — a derived comparison can't).
   **Hover — the full name, in order**: hovering the settled wordmark plays SUQUIA -> (decipher) -> JAVIER (held ~800ms, fully readable) -> (decipher) -> SUAREZ (held ~800ms) -> (decipher) -> SUQUIA (rest) — ~2.8s total. Guard rails: ignored outright while already running (never restarts, never queues) and always runs to completion once started, even if the cursor leaves (there's no mouseleave handler to interrupt it); a 1.5s cooldown after any run ends (arrival's included) before hover can trigger again, so moving the cursor around the name doesn't flicker it; hover-capable devices only, enforced in CSS (`.wordmark-hover-target`'s `pointer-events` stays `none` outside `@media (hover: hover)`, so touch devices never get a valid event target there at all, not just a JS-level no-op); the pointer-events grant is scoped to that one wrapping span around the word (shrink-to-fit), never the `<h1>` itself, which stays `pointer-events-none` and spans the full viewport.
   Everything here lives entirely as descendants of the blended `<h1>` (`mix-blend-mode: difference` stays on the `<h1>` itself, unchanged from before) — every position, every width transition, only ever adds new descendants, never a new ancestor, which is the one thing that's broken this blend before; verified over a photo during a scramble, during both holds, and at rest. `prefers-reduced-motion`: renders "SUQUIA" directly, no scramble on arrival, no hover sequence.
7. **Letter scramble** (`src/motion/ScrambleText.tsx`) — hover-only, on the small tracked-caps nav labels only (`ABOUT ME`, `ALBUMS`, `ATLAS`, `CREDITS`; never serif titles, captions, album names, or the wordmark, which has its own unrelated effect above). Each character cycles through a few random Latin letters (uppercase A–Z only — no symbols, digits, or other scripts; the visual case is moot either way, since these render through a CSS `uppercase` on an ancestor) before landing on its own final letter, resolving left to right, ~220ms total. Each character SLOT's width is locked (via `offsetWidth`, measured client-side — there's no CSS "tabular figures" equivalent for general letters the way `tabular-nums` covers digits) to its own final letter's natural width, re-measured once fonts finish loading. `offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight`, never `getBoundingClientRect()`, is the convention for this kind of measurement everywhere in this codebase: `getBoundingClientRect()` reports the axis-aligned box AFTER any ancestor transform, and for the rotated `CREDITS` label it was reporting the element's true HEIGHT into the `width` field, which then got written back as the element's own CSS width and compounded on every remeasure, shifting the whole label's rotated position — `offsetWidth` is a pre-transform layout property and doesn't have this failure mode. The rotated `CREDITS` label needs no other special handling for its vertical axis — locking width in the text's own pre-rotation coordinate space is exactly equivalent to a fixed height once `-rotate-90` is applied. Verified: all four labels' bounding boxes are identical at rest, mid-scramble, and after settling. Re-entering re-triggers; leaving mid-flight snaps straight to the final text rather than reversing or continuing. `prefers-reduced-motion`: no scramble at all.

Anti-motion: no cursor-parallax on layout (cursor may grow over interactive images, never move the page). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Photo treatment

Photos in **color always** — no B&W-until-hover. Focus within groups via dim/scale of inactive items, never desaturation of the work.

## Place data (per photo)

Every photo carries a `place` (`src/data/photos.ts`, `Place` type): `city`, `country`, `lat`/`lng` (`number | null`), `precision` (`'exact' | 'city'`). Feeds both the album caption (above) and the atlas (§ below). Rules:

- `lat`/`lng` are written by hand — never derived from EXIF, which these files almost certainly don't have.
- `precision: 'exact'` means `lat`/`lng` are the real spot; `precision: 'city'` means Javi didn't want the exact spot published and `lat`/`lng` hold the **city centroid** instead, not a real location. The map code never branches on which — it just plots whatever's there.
- `precision` governs *display* only: `'exact'` may be shown as precise degree/minute/second coordinates (atlas hover, never the caption); `'city'` must never be — that would claim a precision the data doesn't have.
- `lat`/`lng` may be `null` for anything not yet filled in. Every consumer tolerates that: the atlas simply omits the photo (no placeholder mark), and the caption shows "City, Country" alone regardless — the caption never shows coordinates at all, at either precision.
- Filled in by hand across all 32 photos (México, Panamá, New York, Spain, and the south of France). A handful (Puerto Escondido, San Blas) are still `lat`/`lng: null` and are simply absent from the atlas until filled in.

## Hard constraints

- White background; grayscale UI; photos are the only color.
- No cropping (carousel slivers excepted).
- Every photo fully visible within the viewport.
- Desktop perfect; mobile good (dedicated simpler mobile landing if the scatter doesn't translate).
- Performance: transform/opacity-only animation; 60fps on mid-range mobile; Lighthouse LCP < 2.5s.

Dated, newest first — kept going forward alongside any change to this spec, so a reader can see not just current behavior (above) but what changed, when, and why. Entries summarize; the Motion inventory and other sections above stay the source of truth for CURRENT mechanics.

- **2026-09-22 — Decipher wordmark: geometric collision fix.** The width-aware scramble pool (Motion inventory #6) had three compounding bugs, found via a `Range`-based glyph-advance-box measurement (each rendered character's true ink extent, checked for overlap with its neighbour across full arrival and hover runs): (1) an empty-pool fallback that showed the transition's TARGET letter even when the slot hadn't yet tweened wide enough to fit it; (2) glyph cycling on its own `setInterval`, which under load can fire later than its nominal delay, leaving a letter on screen past the point its own width no longer fit the (independently CSS-tweening) slot; (3) the pool's upper ratio (originally 1.12x, matching a literal formula given at the time) permitting genuine ~10px overflow for wide letters in wide slots, since slots sit edge-to-edge with no gap. Fixed by unifying width and glyph-cycling under one `requestAnimationFrame` loop reading a single analytic elapsed-time function (no more independently-drifting CSS transition and interval), and tightening the pool's upper ratio to 1.0 (zero intentional overflow) — the user's explicit choice among three options offered, trading some "breathing" variety on wide slots for a verified guarantee. Confirmed: max measured overlap 0.375-0.7px across repeated runs (down from ~22px before any of the three fixes).
- **2026-09-22 — Wordmark: drum -> decipher, sequencing inverted to name-first.** Replaced the mechanical drum turn (3D `rotateX` per letter) with the DECIPHER effect described in Motion inventory #6 — `src/motion/ReelWordmark.tsx` deleted, `src/motion/DecipherWordmark.tsx` is the new home. Also inverted the landing's own sequencing: the wordmark's scramble now IS the loading indicator (starts immediately, keeps scrambling until textures are ready, then locks to SUQUIA), and the photo entrance waits on that lock (`onArrived`) — every prior model had this backwards, the name waiting on the photos.
- **2026-09-21 — Wordmark: split-flap -> reel -> drum (three motion models in one session, none live today).** In sequence: a split-flap board (JAVIER->SUAREZ->SUQUIA holds, cycling through fixed-width columns) with sequencing/timing/hover-replay fixes on the original build; superseded by a continuous scrolling REEL (slot-machine/odometer style, ~10 random filler letters before landing on each target); superseded by the mechanical DRUM described in the entry above (no random filler, only the three real name-letters per column, identical letters held still between states). Also this session: photo-entrance-relative wordmark sequencing (the wordmark waiting on the photo entrance's own tween — later fully inverted, see 2026-09-22), the nav-label letter-scramble (`src/motion/ScrambleText.tsx`, Motion inventory #7), and the CREDITS label position regression — root-caused to `getBoundingClientRect()` reporting a rotated element's POST-transform box instead of its true pre-rotation size, fixed by switching to `offsetWidth` everywhere in this codebase, on principle.
