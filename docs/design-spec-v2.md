# Design Spec v2 — SUQUIA Photography Portfolio

Supersedes v1. Reflects the decisions made over the mockup iterations (canvas: three approved artboards — Landing, Album carousel, Album page).

## Concept statement

**A white, quiet, editorial page where the photographs are the only color.** Serif typography in a grayscale hierarchy, generous whitespace, and motion as the personality layer: an inverted name over drifting photos, an endless calm carousel, a tube-roll scroll. Nothing competes with the images: no cropping, no symmetric grids, no cursor-following gimmicks.

## Art direction

**Color** — Background near-white `#FAFAFA`. UI text grayscale only: `#111` primary, `#666`–`#888` secondary, `#B0B0B0`–`#CCC` tertiary. No accent color — the photos are the accent.

**Typography** — Serif, in the tamakiyoshida direction: classical but softer than Times. **EB Garamond** (Google Fonts; weights 400/500/600 + italic) is the working face; fallback stack `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`. Usage rules: titles in Title Case ("Alpes", not "ALPES") at weight 500; photo titles in italic ("Ibón de Plan"); small labels uppercase with wide tracking (0.16–0.22em) at 12–13px; numbers at 14px, `#666`. Importance is expressed by size + gray value, never by weight ramping.

**Brand** — The name **SUQUIA** appears: huge on the landing (~158px, weight 500), small in headers/footer (13px, tracked uppercase). No monogram/mark. Primary nav labels: `ABOUT ME`, `ALBUMS` top-left, `ATLAS` top-right (split from the other two — a view over the whole archive, not a section); one rotated 90° edge label, `CREDITS`, on the landing's left edge (a deliberate asymmetry — see §1).

**Layout principles** — Photos always at natural aspect ratio, never cropped (the only exception: carousel slivers, which are teasers by design). Organic scatter, not grids. Every photo fits within one viewport height (~max 85vh) — never larger than the screen.

## Page structure

### 1. Landing
**A tall scrolling composition (~250–300vh), smoothed with Lenis — superseded from an earlier "single fixed screen" draft.** The page genuinely scrolls; reference: gregorcollienne.com. **SUQUIA stays fixed in the center of the viewport for the whole scroll**, as do the nav, rotated edge labels, and counter — none of them are part of the scrolling layer. The photos (9–10 of them, mostly favorites, one or two acting as album shortcuts) live in the scrolling layer and pass behind/around the fixed name as the page scrolls; spaced so 2–3 are on screen at a time, natural aspect ratios always, each ≤ 70vh. On desktop every photo keeps clear whitespace around it (min ~60px to its neighbors); at narrow/compressed widths some overlap is acceptable. Each photo and its number (`01`, `02`, …) are wrapped in the same container so they move together in sync — the number sits just outside the photo edge (below-left), never covered. **SUQUIA renders white with `mix-blend-mode: difference`** — reads near-black on the `#FAFAFA` background at rest and inverts wherever a photo passes under it. This depends on strict stacking-context hygiene: no `transform`/`opacity`/`filter`/`will-change`/`perspective` on the fixed name element's ancestors, or on the scrolling layer's own wrapper — only individual photos animate, never the name or anything between it and the page root. (This exact setup broke once already from an ancestor transform; verify on load, with no photo under it, that the name reads black before treating this as done.) Directly beneath the name, ~30px below its baseline: a fixed, centered descriptor line, `SELECTED PHOTOGRAPHS THROUGH THE YEARS`, 13px, letter-spacing 0.34em, plain `#888888` — a sibling of the `<h1>`, not text inside it, so it never inherits the blend. It never scrolls and is never covered by a photo at any scroll position (guaranteed by z-index alone: it sits in the same z-30 layer as the name, above the z-10 photo layer, regardless of horizontal position — no `LANDING_LAYOUT` exclusion needed the way the name's own blend band needs one). Chrome: `ABOUT ME` and `ALBUMS` top-left, `ATLAS` top-right (13px, tracking 0.22em, `#111111` resting — the same treatment as the other two; deliberately split from them since Atlas is a view over the whole archive, not a section like the other two), a single rotated `CREDITS` label on the *left* edge (clear of the photos, links to `/credits`), `01 / 06` counter bottom — all fixed, no scroll hint. (A matching `MENU` label was removed — it drove no overlay or state, just a label with nothing behind it. `CREDITS` alone is a deliberate asymmetry now, not a leftover; don't add a label back on the other edge to "balance" it. `PHOTOGRAPHS — {year}` was also removed from the top-right — non-interactive text sitting among interactive links invited clicks that went nowhere, and the descriptor now does that labeling job.) The ALBUMS nav link is the only way into the carousel; the landing scroll never leads there.

### 2. Album carousel (its own view — NOT reached by scrolling the landing) — BUILT, `src/motion/AlbumsCarousel.tsx`
Opened only via the `ALBUMS` nav link. **Infinite and circular** — the strip loops; there is no first or last position (indices are `mod`'d into the album list, not clamped). Anatomy as actually implemented:

- **Slides**: constant height (500px) always, for every slide, at every moment — nothing ever scales vertically. A folded slide is a fixed, equal-width sliver (110px) regardless of its album's aspect ratio; the open (active) slide's width is its cover's own natural width at that height (`height × aspect ratio`) — landscape covers open wide, vertical covers open as a tall panel, and the width animates between those two states, never the height. Covers are never cropped.
- **Commit is position-triggered, not velocity-triggered**: scroll/drag feed a velocity that integrates into an offset; crossing one slot's pitch (sliver width + gap) advances the active index by one and the offset carries the remainder — no minimum speed, no separate "flick" threshold. Below that distance the strip simply follows the input 1:1 and holds wherever it's left, with **no snap-back or rubber-banding** — recentring happens only as a side effect of an actual commit, never while idle.
- **Direction-based edge anchoring**: advancing forward pins the open slide's RIGHT edge in place and reveals/grows it toward the left (the image is pinned `right:0`, cropped/revealed from its left side); advancing backward mirrors this exactly (LEFT edge pinned, grows right, image pinned `left:0`). Both the outgoing and incoming slide in a given step always share the same anchor side, so a commit reads as one continuous motion rather than two independent folds.
- **Rest position is two fixed lines, not the viewport centre**: `X_RIGHT`/`X_LEFT` are computed once from the *average* natural cover width across all albums and never move. A forward commit settles with the open slide's right edge exactly on `X_RIGHT`; a backward commit settles with its left edge exactly on `X_LEFT`. The open photo's own centre therefore varies a little album to album (narrower covers rest right-of-centre, wider ones left-of-centre) — accepted as the natural consequence of a genuinely static anchor edge, not corrected for.
- **Frozen image anchoring**: which edge a slide's `<img>` is pinned to is decided once — when that slide starts folding or unfolding, or when it first mounts if it's never been opened — and held fixed for as long as it stays folded. A settled sliver's crop never changes again on its own; only the slide actually transitioning right now may pick a new anchor.
- **Depth cues**: blur and a dark veil scale by distance from the active index (a few fixed steps, not a continuous gradient) — the active slide has neither.
- **No native scroll on either axis** — the page and the strip are both `overflow: hidden`; all motion is transform-driven, never real scroll position.
- **Navigation**: only the open (active) slide is a link, to `/album/[slug]` — folded slivers do nothing on click, only step the carousel via the normal drag/scroll physics. Hovering the open slide nudges the info-block title ~4px right and grows a thin rule under it (300ms, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`); nothing on the photo itself ever moves or transforms. This is a plain client-side route change today — the shared-element "jump into" expansion (Motion inventory #4) is not built yet.

Info bottom-left (album title serif ~30px + gray meta line), pagination bottom-right (`01 / 04`).

### 3. Album page
**No hero.** The carousel's jump-into transition (center slide expands, GSAP Flip) IS the entrance; the page opens on a typographic title block: small `ALBUM 01 — 12 PHOTOS`, title serif ~110px, location/year line. Then a vertical top-down flow: photos at natural ratios, each ≤ one viewport height, alternating placement (centered / offset-left / offset-right), each with info alongside. **Caption hierarchy — place outranks technical data:**
```
01   San Miguel Street   ·   San Miguel de Allende, México
                              43MM — F/5.6 — ISO 100
```
Number (`#BBBBBB`), italic serif title (`#111111`), and place (`#666666`, "City, Country" — never coordinates, at any precision; that's an atlas-hover thing, not a caption thing) share one baseline row; capture data (focal length — aperture — ISO) drops to its own line below at 12px `#A8A8A8`, the footnote. Wraps gracefully at narrow widths. Right-edge fixed control: SVG chevron up / `01 / 12` counter / chevron down for prev/next photo navigation. Ends with a NEXT ALBUM block (small cover + title) and a minimal footer (SUQUIA — INSTAGRAM — EMAIL — year).

### 4. About and Credits — two separate pages, not one
Split after the original single-page plan proved too cramped for both a personal bio and a colophon; each now has its own route.

**4a. `/about`** — One column, generous whitespace, same typographic system, no images competing (an optional portrait slot exists but stays commented out until a real photo is supplied). Contents: a short bio in EB Garamond; contact structured as its own labelled block (email as plain selectable text with a `mailto:` link — no contact form, no backend, no third-party mail service; the block is scoped so a form could replace the email later without touching anything else on the page) and a separate Instagram block in the same label+value pattern. Same "← Suquia" back-link chrome as the album page. Reachable from the header nav (`ABOUT ME`).

**4b. `/credits`** — A colophon, not a page about the photographer. Small quiet type, one column, plenty of air, no heading shouting "Credits" in large type — the page should read as a footnote. Exactly five lines: the photography/design/development credit, the typeface, the "built with" stack, a GitHub repository link, and a rights line ("All photographs © Javier Suquia, {year}. Not to be reproduced without permission.", year from one computed constant). Not in the header nav — reached via the landing's rotated `CREDITS` edge label (see §1's chrome note: `MENU` was removed as a dead label, so `CREDITS` is now the site's only rotated edge label, a deliberate asymmetry).

### 5. Atlas (`/atlas`) — a map over the whole archive, reached from the header's `ATLAS` link

**Not the tube-roll site, not a gallery.** An orthographic GLOBE, hand-rolled on a single `<canvas>` — no map library, no three.js/GL — the header (`Atlas` serif title + a meta line, counts computed from `photos.ts` at render time, never hardcoded: `{N} Photographs — {N} Cities — {N} Countries`) sits above a full-bleed canvas view (`src/motion/Atlas.tsx`). Superseded a flat equirectangular SVG map (see the Changelog for why: its dot lattice was baked once in world coordinates and scaled with zoom, so zooming in just spread the same dots further apart rather than resolving more coastline).

- **Projection** — orthographic, camera at infinity: `cosc = sin(phi0)·sin(phi) + cos(phi0)·cos(phi)·cos(lambda-lambda0)`; skip any point with `cosc < 0` (far hemisphere); `x = R·cos(phi)·sin(lambda-lambda0)`, `y = R·(cos(phi0)·sin(phi) - sin(phi0)·cos(phi)·cos(lambda-lambda0))`; screen position is `(cx+x, cy-y)`. `lambda0`/`phi0` are the view's own rotation, `R` the radius in CSS px. Anything with `cosc` under 0.15 fades linearly to 0 so nothing pops into existence at the limb.
- **Landmass** — sampled at RUNTIME from a monochrome equirectangular raster mask (`public/atlas/land-mask.png`, 2048×1024, generated by `scripts/generate-atlas-land-mask.mjs` from the same Natural Earth 110m source the old flat map used, Public Domain — see `docs/atlas-data-sources.md`), decoded once into a plain `Uint8Array` on mount. The dot LATTICE itself is rebuilt whenever the radius `R` has drifted more than 15% since the last build, at an angular step `delta = TARGET_PX / R` (`TARGET_PX` = 7px) — latitude steps by `delta`, longitude by `delta / cos(phi)` so the spacing is uniform on the SPHERE SURFACE (dots naturally compress toward the limb under the projection — that's the curvature cue, kept deliberately, not corrected for). This is the actual fix: on-screen dot spacing stays pinned to `TARGET_PX` at every zoom level instead of degrading linearly the way the flat map's fixed lattice did. `#CCCCCC`, drawn as small filled rects (cheaper per-call than arc+fill at ~3000+ dots/frame).
- **Marks** — one per photo that has coordinates; photos without are simply omitted, no placeholder, and the globe renders correctly and calmly with zero marks (picking them up automatically as `photos.ts` gains coordinates — no wiring beyond the existing `page.tsx` → `AtlasMark[]` mapping). Projected with the same formula, culled/faded the same way. `precision: 'exact'` → solid filled dot, `#111111`. `precision: 'city'` → unfilled ring, same stroke colour (still filled with the background colour underneath, not `"none"`, so the whole disc — not just the ring line — is the hit target; canvas has no native per-shape hit-testing, so this is handled by hand in `findClusterAt`, checking distance from the cursor against every projected mark's screen position each frame). Marks within 14 screen px of each other cluster into one numbered mark, recomputed every frame (cheap at this N — the archive has a few dozen marks, not thousands). One-line legend, caption grey, bottom-left.
- **Navigation is rotation only — no panning.** Drag: `dx → lambda0 -= dx·(π/R)`, `dy → phi0 += dy·(π/R)`, `phi0` clamped to ±70° so the globe never tips past the poles. Inertia: velocity carries on past release (0.94 friction/frame, stops below 0.0002 rad/frame). Idle drift: after 4s with no interaction, `lambda0` rotates at 1.5°/s until any pointer input cancels it — armed by a `setTimeout` rescheduled on every real interaction, NOT by checking elapsed time inside the render loop (the loop only runs while something is actively moving and stops entirely when idle, so a time-check living inside it could never notice the 4s had passed once it had already gone quiet — the setTimeout is what wakes the stopped loop back up). `prefers-reduced-motion`: idle drift never arms at all. Zoom: wheel/pinch, `R` between 0.32× and 1.6× the shorter viewport dimension; density stays correct throughout since the lattice resamples. Default framing: `lambda0 = -46°`, `phi0 = 26°` — Mexico, Panama, New York, Spain and the south of France all visible on the face at once. "Reset View" (bottom-right) animates back to it.
- **Hover** — shows the photo small beside its mark, with `CITY · COUNTRY`, and for `'exact'` marks only, DMS coordinates; the hovered mark also enlarges slightly with a thin ring. Click on a single mark animates `lambda0`/`phi0` along the shortest angular path to centre it (600ms ease-out, computed via each cluster member's own unit-vector average so the path is always the short way around, never crossing the far side) then navigates to that photo inside its album (`/album/[slug]#photo-N`); click on a CLUSTER centres the same way, then opens a small panel listing every photo in it (thumbnail + city/country, each a direct link) rather than the old flat map's "just zoom in" behaviour, since a cluster's own members can be scattered too far apart on a rotated globe for one more zoom step to reliably separate them.
- **Performance** — one `requestAnimationFrame` loop, running only while rotating, zooming, drifting, or mid-recenter; stops outright (no polling, no idle ticking) the instant nothing is moving. One canvas pass per frame: clear, dots, marks — no per-dot DOM, ever. Verified at 60fps while continuously dragging at a 1440×900 viewport.

## Motion inventory (7 effects — #1-6 frozen from the original approved set; #7, the nav-label scramble, added later as a small hover-only polish pass, not a departure from "frozen")

1. **Tube/roll scroll** (Lenis + WebGL2 via `ogl`, not GSAP ScrollTrigger — superseded after prototyping in `/lab/tube`) — every photo on a page is painted onto one shared canvas (`src/motion/GLSurface.tsx`, which mirrors the page's normal `<img>` elements rather than replacing them) and bends around a cylinder belonging to the *viewport*, not any individual photo: zero displacement at the screen's vertical center, maximum at the top/bottom edges, driven by scroll velocity and direction-independent (scrolling up and down bend the same way). Site-wide signature, but with per-instance intensity — spectacle-level on the landing, barely perceptible on album pages (the work should be looked at there, not performed at) — via the same component's `bendDepth`/`bendRadiusMultiplier` props. Falls back to the plain DOM/CSS photos if a WebGL context can't be created; `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps GL active but pins the bend flat. The carousel does not use this — its motion model is independent (see §2). On the landing, Lenis smooths the real page scroll and each photo also gets a modest depth-based parallax on top of it (unchanged, still CSS transform, independent of the GL layer); the fixed name is never part of that transformed layer.
2. **Hover on images** — inner zoom/shift within a fixed frame; boundaries never move.
3. **Infinite carousel** — circular drag/scroll strip, slivers + open center.
4. **Jump-into transition** — carousel center expands into the album page (GSAP Flip / View Transitions).
5. **Rolling digits** — pagination and photo counters animate per-digit.
6. **Entrance reveals** — on the landing only, on first load and every soft-navigation back to `/`: every photo whose FINAL rest position lands within the viewport (expanded 15% of the viewport height on each side — computed from the photo's own aspect ratio and its REAL on-screen width, including the `<900px` responsive width bump `globals.css` applies to `.landing-photo`, never the authored placement width alone) starts stacked at the viewport centre, hidden, and eases out (750ms, ease-out cubic, staggered ~10ms apart from the centre of the composition outward). Every OTHER photo — never near the viewport at arrival — renders straight at its final position, opacity 1, untouched: nothing about it moves or fades. Opacity and transform travel on the SAME timeline: 0 at the start, ramping to 1 over the first 40% of an item's own travel, never reaching 1 while the transform is still near its full offset. The starting state for a participating photo — `opacity: 0` plus the centre-ward offset — is declared directly in the photo's JSX (computed analytically from its own layout data, in vw/vh units, not measured) so it's present in the very first rendered HTML; the reveal effect itself is idempotent per arrival (latched against replaying twice for the same `arriveKey`, regardless of how many renders satisfy its guard) and recomputes on window resize, so a viewport change mid-flight never leaves stale pixel offsets. The parallax offset and the entrance offset live on two separate nested wrapper elements (outer = parallax, always live from frame one, `transition: none` explicit in CSS; inner = entrance, animates to exactly `translate(0,0)` and opacity `1` and is then left alone, also `transition: none` explicit) — both wrappers' animation is 100% JS/rAF-driven, so an explicit `none` heads off a real hazard: either element inheriting a `transition-duration` later (even from an unrelated shared utility class) would make every one of these per-frame imperative writes kick off its own CSS transition on top of the already-eased JS value. The GLSurface tube/roll renderer reads each mirrored node's *parent's* `opacity` as a per-plane alpha multiplier each frame (`uOpacity`, fragment-shader-side) — a DOM `opacity: 0` alone hides only the `<img>`, not what the canvas paints from its live rect, so this is required for the hidden state to actually be invisible on screen, not just in the DOM. `prefers-reduced-motion`: no travel, photos simply fade in (opacity only) at their final position, gated on texture-readiness directly.
   The wordmark plays its own arrival (`src/motion/DecipherWordmark.tsx`) starting 600ms AFTER the photo entrance begins — staggered deliberately so the two effects read as sequential beats, not simultaneous, rather than one gating the other (see the 2026-09-23 changelog entry below for why this replaced an earlier "wordmark IS the loading state, photos wait on its lock" model).
   **The wordmark's letters never change after this point — see the 2026-09-26 changelog entry for why.** Five separate mechanical letter-change mechanisms (a split-flap board, a continuous reel, a mechanical drum, then a ring-based split-flap board again) were built, tested, and torn out across two sessions: JAVIER/SUAREZ/SUQUIA measure 534.4 / 603.0 / 595.2px and share almost no glyph advances in EB Garamond (I 55.0px vs Q 120.4px, a 2.2x spread), so any mechanical effect needs a fixed module a proportional serif simply doesn't have. The wordmark now does exactly one animated thing, ever:
   **DECIPHER** (arrival only — means "not resolved yet"): each of the 6 positions cycles through random letters — drawn ONLY from the letters of Javi's own name (J A V I E R S U Z Q) — then locks on its own target letter of SUQUIA; positions lock left to right, cascading over ~600ms total with a 300ms spread. Width geometry: EB Garamond's capitals are proportional (widest letter ~2.2x the narrowest at this size), so each position's own box width tweens on the same per-position clock as its glyph lock, and the random pool a position draws from every tick is filtered to letters whose own measured width is `>= 0.6x` and `<= 1.0x` the slot's width at that instant — both read from ONE analytic function of elapsed time, driven by a single `requestAnimationFrame` loop. Since decipher resolves directly to SUQUIA now (it used to resolve to JAVIER, with three more words and two more mechanisms following), source and target width are both SUQUIA's own — the interpolation still runs the same formula, it just always evaluates to the same value, which is the simplest way to leave the mechanism's own timing untouched while removing everything downstream of it. Letter widths are measured at runtime via `offsetWidth` from hidden reference spans, re-measured on mount, resize, and `document.fonts.ready`.
   Once decipher settles, the per-column structure swaps for the plain static "SUQUIA" text node and never changes again for the rest of that page load. Everything mid-run is `aria-hidden`; the wordmark's accessible name comes from `aria-label="Suquia"` on the `<h1>` itself. `prefers-reduced-motion`: arrival never plays at all — the wordmark renders "SUQUIA" directly from the first frame and stays that way.
   **Hover reveals a caption, not a letter change.** Hovering the wordmark fades in a single static line — "JAVIER SUAREZ SUQUIA," the full name as a plain sentence, not a second logotype — centered underneath it: 13px, uppercase, 0.18em letter-spacing, weight 400, 1.25em below the wordmark's own baseline box. Opacity 0->1 plus a 4px->0 translateY over 200ms ease-out on enter, opacity->0 over 160ms on leave; no letter animation of any kind. It's absolutely positioned relative to the wordmark's own (position:relative, otherwise unchanged) root, so it can never shift the wordmark's layout — verified: SUQUIA at rest measures the exact same box (437.17px from viewport left, 565.66px wide, at 158px reference) with and without the caption present. It lives inside the same blended `<h1>` as the wordmark (a descendant, not an ancestor of it — see the mix-blend-mode note below) and gets its own opacity/transform directly, which is safe for the same reason the decipher columns' own width-tweening always was: only a new ANCESTOR between the h1 and the page root breaks the blend, never a descendant. `aria-hidden` — the name is already announced by the h1's `aria-label` and the About page. `prefers-reduced-motion`: the caption still appears on hover, just with `transition: none` (instant show/hide). Verified over real photos across light, dark, and medium/hazy tones (foliage silhouettes, night sky, sea/sky blues, building facades) — legible in every case; a synthetic flat-#808080 test does render it very close to invisible (mix-blend-mode: difference's mathematically worst case — white-on-128-gray differences to 127, a 1-part-in-255 gap from the background it's sitting on), but this is an intrinsic property of the blend mode itself, equally true of the wordmark's own letters, independent of font size, and not one any of the actual 32 photos triggers.
7. **Letter scramble** (`src/motion/ScrambleText.tsx`) — hover-only, on the small tracked-caps nav labels only (`ABOUT ME`, `ALBUMS`, `ATLAS`, `CREDITS`; never serif titles, captions, album names, or the wordmark, which has its own unrelated effect above). Each character cycles through a few random Latin letters (uppercase A–Z only — no symbols, digits, or other scripts; the visual case is moot either way, since these render through a CSS `uppercase` on an ancestor) before landing on its own final letter, resolving left to right, ~220ms total. Each character SLOT's width is locked (via `offsetWidth`, measured client-side — there's no CSS "tabular figures" equivalent for general letters the way `tabular-nums` covers digits) to its own final letter's natural width, re-measured once fonts finish loading. `offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight`, never `getBoundingClientRect()`, is the convention for this kind of measurement everywhere in this codebase: `getBoundingClientRect()` reports the axis-aligned box AFTER any ancestor transform, and for the rotated `CREDITS` label it was reporting the element's true HEIGHT into the `width` field, which then got written back as the element's own CSS width and compounded on every remeasure, shifting the whole label's rotated position — `offsetWidth` is a pre-transform layout property and doesn't have this failure mode. The rotated `CREDITS` label needs no other special handling for its vertical axis — locking width in the text's own pre-rotation coordinate space is exactly equivalent to a fixed height once `-rotate-90` is applied. Verified: all four labels' bounding boxes are identical at rest, mid-scramble, and after settling. Re-entering re-triggers; leaving mid-flight snaps straight to the final text rather than reversing or continuing. `prefers-reduced-motion`: no scramble at all.

Anti-motion: no cursor-parallax on layout (cursor may grow over interactive images, never move the page). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Photo treatment

Photos in **color always** — no B&W-until-hover. Focus within groups via dim/scale of inactive items, never desaturation of the work.

## Place data (per photo)

Every photo carries a `place` (`src/data/photos.ts`, `Place` type): `city`, `country`, `lat`/`lng` (`number | null`), `precision` (`'exact' | 'city'`). Feeds both the album caption (above) and the atlas (§ below). Rules:

- `lat`/`lng` are written by hand — never derived from EXIF, which these files almost certainly don't have.
- `precision: 'exact'` means `lat`/`lng` are the real spot; `precision: 'city'` means Javi didn't want the exact spot published and `lat`/`lng` hold the **city centroid** instead, not a real location. The map code never branches on which — it just plots whatever's there.
- `precision` governs *display* only: `'exact'` may be shown as precise degree/minute/second coordinates (atlas hover, never the caption); `'city'` must never be — that would claim a precision the data doesn't have.
- `lat`/`lng` may be `null` for anything not yet filled in. Every consumer tolerates that: the atlas simply omits the photo (no placeholder mark), and the caption shows "City, Country" alone regardless — the caption never shows coordinates at all, at either precision.
- Filled in by hand across all 32 photos (México, Panamá, New York, Spain, and the south of France). A handful (Puerto Escondido, San Blas) are still `lat`/`lng: null` and are simply absent from the atlas until filled in.

## Hard constraints

- White background; grayscale UI; photos are the only color.
- No cropping (carousel slivers excepted).
- Every photo fully visible within the viewport.
- Desktop perfect; mobile good (dedicated simpler mobile landing if the scatter doesn't translate).
- Performance: transform/opacity-only animation; 60fps on mid-range mobile; Lighthouse LCP < 2.5s.

- **2026-09-25 — Wordmark: drum replaced with split-flap (FLAP); a real double-arrival bug and several geometry bugs found and fixed during verification.** The three-stop drum (Motion inventory #6, always exactly one notch per transition regardless of distance) replaced with a proper split-flap board: a 10-letter ring, shortest-path travel per column (see Motion inventory #6 for the full mechanics), no boxes/backgrounds, reduced-motion crossfade fallback. Several real bugs found via testing, not just implementation from spec:
  - **`LandingComposition`'s `arriveKey` could bump twice for one activation.** Its bump effect (`if (!active) return; setArriveKey(k => k+1)`) had no guard against React Strict Mode's dev-only double-invoke of layout effects, so a single arrival could increment `arriveKey` 0->1->2 instead of 0->1. Since the wordmark's own arrival-trigger effect only guards against re-scheduling the SAME `arriveKey`, a second, genuinely-different key read as a real new arrival and replayed the whole decipher/flap sequence mid-hover — confirmed live: it silently broke the hover-leave transition by stomping `arrivalActiveRef` back to `true`. Fixed with a ref latch (`arriveBumpedForActiveRef`) that only resets when `active` goes false, so a genuine return visit still replays correctly.
  - **The flap/fade structures' outer wrapper had no vertical-align, unlike decipher's own (working) columns.** `data-flap-live`/`data-fade-live` are `display:inline-block` wrappers whose only children are `position:absolute` (required for the reserved-width mechanic) — with no in-flow content, an inline-block's default alignment falls back to "bottom margin edge at the line's baseline," not the line's top, which put the whole flap structure roughly 150px below where the real rest text (and decipher's own columns, which use `vertical-align: top` explicitly) actually render. Fixed by adding the same `vertical-align: top` decipher already uses.
  - **The hardcoded per-letter advance table was ~5% off this project's actual live-rendered metrics** (confirmed via canvas `measureText` independent of the DOM), enough to visibly misalign FLAP's own rest position from the real settled text and to compute wrong reserved widths. Fixed by switching every FLAP geometry calculation to the same live `offsetWidth` measurement decipher's own alphabet-scramble-pool already performs, instead of the static table (kept only as a same-tick fallback before the first measurement runs).
  - **Position tweening toward the real (narrower) rest position while a column was still at its constant reserved width mathematically guaranteed overlap** whenever reserved width exceeds real width — confirmed via `Range`-based glyph-ink measurement (not just bounding-box measurement, which over-counts a masked/overflow-hidden flap's full un-clipped layout box) to reach up to ~50px on real transitions, on both the open and close sides of the tween. Fixed by holding position at the reserved-width tiling for the whole transition and swapping width+position to the real target as a discrete jump, gated on every column simultaneously — see Motion inventory #6.
  - **A stale moving-upper flap could stay visible after its own step.** Its `onfinish` handler reset it back to ITS OWN starting pose (flat, opaque) rather than pinning it at where it had just finished (rotated away, hidden); since fixed-upper already carries the correct current letter throughout a step, this made the flap's own now-stale "old letter" text reappear fully opaque whenever that step was the last one in its column's run — for a column whose old letter happened to be wide, this visibly overlapped its neighbor for the rest of the run. Fixed by pinning the reset to the flap's actual just-finished pose.
  - **A slow column's flip-step chain could still be mid-flight when the shared position tween's declared duration elapsed**, because each ring-step is a separately-created, sequentially-chained WAAPI animation (a new one only created inside the previous one's `onfinish`), and the real dispatch latency between chained calls — confirmed via testing to run 1.5-3x the declared per-step duration under load (GL rendering elsewhere on the page) — compounds across a column with several steps in a way `computeTransitionTotalMs`'s pure-math estimate doesn't account for. Fixed by gating each column's atomic width/position snap on its own flip-chain promise actually resolving, not just the shared position tween's own `totalMs`.
  - `react-hooks/immutability` lint error on a "pending action, consumed by a seeding layout effect" ref pattern that worked in the prior (drum-era) version of this file — resolved not by chasing the exact trigger but by removing the pattern altogether: the double-`requestAnimationFrame` paint-boundary wait (still required — a single rAF can land in the same frame as the layout effect that scheduled it) now runs directly from each hover/tap entry point instead of being queued through a ref for the effect to pick up.
- **2026-09-23 — Atlas: canvas-never-sized root cause (a React Strict Mode rAF-id leak, not a missing resize implementation); wordmark: swap replaced with a three-stop drum; a "leaked WebGL context" report traced to the (deliberate, unchanged) persistent-landing architecture, not a real leak.**
  - **Atlas canvas stuck at its 300x150 HTML default**: reported as the canvas never being given an explicit size. Direct instrumentation showed the opposite — a working DPR-aware resize implementation already existed (`draw()`, gated on a `ResizeObserver`-fed `viewportSize`) but its own `draw()` calls were never happening at all: `requestFrame()`'s guard (`if (rafRef.current !== null) return`) was permanently wedged non-null forever after the FIRST scheduled frame, because the unmount-cleanup effect cancelled that frame without resetting the ref to `null`. In production this would only bite on a genuine unmount; in `next dev`, React's Strict Mode double-invokes every effect (mount -> cleanup -> mount) WITHOUT discarding refs between that simulated cleanup and the remount, so the cancelled id from the very first mount wedged the guard for the component's entire real lifetime — every later `requestFrame()` call (ResizeObserver firing, mask loading, etc.) silently no-opped, `tick()` never ran again, `draw()` never ran again, the canvas never got its one-time resize. Fixed by resetting `rafRef.current = null` alongside the `cancelAnimationFrame` call. Also added, as defensive hardening rather than root-cause fixes: an explicit `className="block h-full w-full"` on the canvas (a CSS fallback for the instant before the first real `draw()` call), and re-clamping (not resetting) `R` into `[minR, maxR]` on every resize rather than only seeding it once, so a window shrink can't leave the zoom level sitting outside the now-smaller valid range indefinitely. Verified: `canvas.width/height` now track `container.clientWidth/height * devicePixelRatio` exactly at every viewport size and DPR, confirmed stable (no stretching, no blur) across repeated resizes and repeated full navigation cycles.
  - **"Two `<main>` elements, one orphaned" — investigated, not changed.** The report characterized the persistent landing's own hidden (`display:none`) `<main>` — which stays mounted on every non-`/` route specifically so its WebGL context, textures, and Lenis instance never have to rebuild on navigation (Architecture rules, this doc and CLAUDE.md, in place since early in the project) — as a per-navigation leak, citing 304ms INP as circumstantial evidence. Direct measurement across repeated round-trip navigation (`/` -> `/albums` -> `/` -> `/atlas` -> `/` -> `/albums` -> `/` -> `/atlas`) found the `<main>` count and canvas count both stayed exactly flat (1 or 2, never growing) the entire time, and only ONE WebGL context ever existed, the whole run. That's the intended steady state of the documented architecture, not an accumulating leak, and `GLSurface`'s own `active` prop already stops its paint loop (not just visually, but the actual `requestAnimationFrame` chain) whenever the landing isn't the current route, so the hidden context isn't consuming per-frame work elsewhere either. Implementing "unmount instead of hide" as literally requested would reverse this deliberate, previously-verified design and reintroduce the exact rebuild-on-every-navigation cost it exists to avoid — not done, flagged instead for confirmation.

A single deferring `requestAnimationFrame` wasn't sufficient fix (it can still fire in the same frame as the effect that scheduled it); a double `requestAnimationFrame` was. Verified via computed-style (not inline-style) sampling, since reading `.style.transform` only shows the animation's assigned target, not what's actually painted: the roll visibly overshoots past its target and settles (matching the prescribed easing), and the six columns' own transitions start measurably staggered (~35-50ms apart, matching the 40ms cascade within rAF sampling granularity).

- **2026-09-23 — Atlas: flat SVG map -> orthographic canvas globe, dot-density fix.** Replaced the equirectangular SVG map (Page structure §5) with a hand-rolled orthographic globe on a single `<canvas>` — no map library, no three.js/GL.
